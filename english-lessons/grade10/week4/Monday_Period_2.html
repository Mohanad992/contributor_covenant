Instruction & Modeling — I Do⏱ 8 min

Sequence ≠ chronology

Narrative order: the order in which the author presents events.

Chronological order: the order in which events actually happen in time.

Worked skill example: The workbook excerpt begins at a later time, then refers back to an event that happened earlier. To reconstruct chronology, identify time markers, place markers, and actions, then reorder the events.
Workbook skill page: Analyze Sequence
Instruction & Modeling — I Do⏱ 7 min

Three signals help you reconstruct sequence

WHEN

Time clues

dates, “before,” “after,” “later,” “the next morning,” “for hours”

WHERE

Place clues

village, newsroom, rescue site, hospital, Rolf’s childhood home

WHAT

Action clues

what changes, what causes the next event, and what the character remembers or does

Teacher model: highlight one time clue, circle one place clue, then state the action in a short verb phrase. This converts a dense paragraph into a sequence map.
Guided Practice — We Do⏱ 10 min

Workbook Practice: reconstruct the order

Team directions
  1. Find the time marker.
  2. Find the place marker.
  3. Reduce each event to 5–8 words.
  4. Place events in chronological order.
Do not copy the paragraph. Sequence analysis requires identifying major actions, not every detail.

Independent Practice — You Do⏱ 8 min

Sequence Challenge

Number these events from earliest to latest in the story world. Then explain which events Allende presents through memory rather than in present-time rescue action.

A. Rolf experiences childhood trauma.
B. The volcanic disaster traps Azucena.
C. Rolf reaches the disaster as a reporter.
D. Rescue attempts fail and the pump is delayed.
E. Rolf’s memories surface while he stays with Azucena.
F. The rescue episode reaches its tragic end.
Chronological order: A → B → C → D → E → F. The childhood trauma occurred decades earlier but is revealed later through memory, showing the difference between chronology and narrative order.
Wrap-Up / Reflection⏱ 4 min
