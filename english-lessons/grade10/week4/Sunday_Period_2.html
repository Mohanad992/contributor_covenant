Instruction & Modeling — I Do⏱ 8 min

Characterization is evidence, not a label

Ask four questions:

  • What does the character do?
  • What does the character say?
  • What do we learn about the character’s thoughts/emotions?
  • How does the narrator describe the character?
Worked example: Rolf is described as unusually calm and persistent in dangerous situations. He also wades into the mud to reach Azucena. These details characterize him as controlled, determined, and action-oriented at the beginning.
Source passage: early characterization of Rolf Carlé
Instruction & Modeling — I Do⏱ 7 min

Internal conflict = struggle inside the character

External event: Azucena cannot be freed.

Rolf’s goal: rescue her.

Internal pressure: he begins losing the emotional distance that normally protects him.

Conflict: Rolf must face helplessness and memories he has kept buried.

Notice the change: the same reporter who seemed emotionally protected by the camera gradually becomes personally vulnerable.

Azucena’s situation acts as a trigger: the failed rescue makes Rolf confront pain from his own past.
Later passage: Rolf’s memories surface and his defenses begin to crumble
