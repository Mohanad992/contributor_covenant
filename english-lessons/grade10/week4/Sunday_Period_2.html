How Rolf Carlé changes when professional distance becomes personal

Warm-Up

Warm-Up: What can an action reveal?

A reporter drops his equipment, walks into waist-deep mud, and stays beside a trapped child.

Key idea

Characterization is not a list of adjectives. It is an inference built from what a character says, does, thinks, remembers, and how others respond.

Read a character through four lenses

Words

What does the character say? How does the tone change?

Actions

What choices does the character make under pressure?

Reactions

What emotions or physical responses appear?

Narrator’s comments

What does the narrator tell us directly about the character?

Good analysis moves beyond: “Rolf is brave.”
Better: “Rolf’s decision to enter the mud shows that his professional role gives way to personal responsibility.”

Rolf before the rescue becomes personal

Close read: Rolf’s public identity

“Nothing could stop him …” • “equanimity” • “fortitude”
Direct characterization: the narrator presents Rolf as persistent, controlled, and difficult to shake.
“the lens of the camera … transported him to a different time”
Metaphor: the camera acts like a psychological shield. It lets him witness suffering without fully entering it emotionally.
Inference: his professional calm is real, but it may also protect him from feelings he does not want to face.

What is internal conflict?

External conflict

A struggle between a character and something outside the self: nature, another person, an institution, or a situation.

Internal conflict

A struggle inside the character: fear, guilt, grief, memory, values, or competing desires.

In this story: Rolf begins with an external rescue problem, but the experience awakens an internal struggle he has kept buried.
Instruction & Modeling
Grade 10 • Unit 1: At Home and Away
Failed rescue attempts and Rolf’s voice beginning to change

Close read: the first visible crack

“I could hear his voice break.”
Characterization through reaction: the narrator hears emotion entering the voice of a man known for composure.
“We can’t wait that long!” Rolf shouted.
Shift in tone: controlled reporting becomes urgency and frustration. He is no longer safely detached behind the camera.

Instruction & Modeling
Grade 10 • Unit 1: At Home and Away
Rolf’s buried memories surface

Literary technique: flashback exposes the conflict

“memory poured out” … “the obstacles that had blocked his consciousness”
Metaphor: memory is imagined as something physically blocked and then released. The language suggests that Rolf has spent years containing trauma.
“the visceral terror he had lived as a boy suddenly invaded him”
Internal conflict: the danger in front of him and the fear from childhood collapse into one experience.

Close read: his defenses “crumble”
