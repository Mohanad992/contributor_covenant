Background box from the textbook

What kind of literature is this?

Literary short story / realistic fiction

The textbook explains that the story is a fictional account based on a real disaster. Allende turns a historical tragedy into a literary narrative centered on human response, memory, and suffering.

How to read it: Keep two levels in mind at the same time: what physically happens and what the event reveals about people.

Narration: a first-person narrator watches Rolf’s reporting and comments on him from a personal distance. This matters because the story moves between public images on television and private emotional experience.

Five devices you will meet in the opening pages

Imagery

Language that appeals to sight, sound, smell, touch, or taste.

Ask: What does the detail make me see or feel?

Simile

A comparison using like or as.

Ask: Why this comparison?

Metaphor

A direct comparison that recasts one thing as another.

Ask: What new meaning does it add?

Personification

Giving human action or qualities to something nonhuman.

Foreshadowing

A clue that prepares us for something important later.

Connotation

The feeling or association a word carries beyond its dictionary meaning.

Opening paragraph: Azucena is introduced

Close read: the opening paragraph

“In that vast cemetery … the little girl … became the symbol of the tragedy.”
Metaphor: the disaster site becomes a “cemetery.” The landscape is no longer ordinary ground; it is defined by death and loss.
“the head budding like a black squash from the clay”
Simile + disturbing imagery: “budding” usually suggests growth, but the comparison is grotesque. The contrast makes Azucena’s entrapment feel unnatural and unforgettable.

Disaster description and the word “cataclysm”

Close read: when the landscape becomes active

“the mountain had awakened again”
Personification: the volcano is presented as a sleeping force that has come back to life. It makes the danger feel powerful and almost intentional.
“villages … buried beneath … telluric vomit”
Metaphor + visceral imagery: the earth appears to violently expel mud and debris. The diction is deliberately ugly; it makes the destruction physical and repulsive.

Vocabulary: meaning is not enough — notice the shade

cataclysm

a very destructive event

Stronger than: accident, problem

tenacity

persistent determination

Positive shade: refusal to give up

equanimity

calm self-control in difficulty

Different from: indifference

fortitude

courage and endurance in pain or adversity

commiserate

to express sympathy for someone’s suffering

bureaucratic

connected with administrative rules and procedures

Instruction & Modeling
Grade 10 • Unit 1: At Home and Away
Rolf is introduced through the narrator’s description

Worked example: use context before the glossary

“Nothing could stop him … I was always amazed at his equanimity in the face of danger and suffering.”
Step 1 — nearby clues: “Nothing could stop him” and “in the face of danger.”
Step 2 — part of speech: a noun naming a quality.
Step 3 — best meaning: calm composure under pressure.
Shade of meaning: equanimity is not simply “calm.” It suggests emotional steadiness when calm would be difficult.
