Conflict is more specific than “a problem”

Person vs. Nature

mud, water, cold, darkness, unstable ground

Person vs. Institution / Society

limited transport, unavailable equipment, administrative obstacles

Person vs. Self

fear, grief, guilt, memory, emotional defenses

Analytical question: Which external conflict merely blocks the rescue, and which one changes Rolf internally?
Instruction & Modeling
Grade 10 • Unit 1: At Home and Away
Rolf tries ropes, poles, soldiers, and needs a pump

Model 1: Person vs. Nature

The mud acts “like quicksand”; Azucena cannot move; the water must be drained.
Evidence: physical conditions trap Azucena.
Conflict: person vs. nature / environment.
Effect on Rolf: repeated failure turns technical problem-solving into frustration and emotional involvement.

Grade 10 • Unit 1: At Home and Away
Administrative obstacles and the shortage of practical help

Model 2: Person vs. Institution

The narration refers to “bureaucratic obstacles” while doctors and supplies cannot solve the central rescue problem.
External conflict: Rolf faces systems that move slowly while Azucena’s situation is urgent.
Why it matters: the conflict increases helplessness. Rolf can report, plead, and ask for equipment, but he cannot make the system respond at the speed the crisis requires.

Model 3: Person vs. Self

“memory poured out” … “the visceral terror he had lived as a boy suddenly invaded him”
Internal conflict: Rolf confronts memories he has kept out of conscious thought.
Important: the flashback is not a separate side story. It is the psychological consequence of the rescue situation.

Close Reading: public promises vs. private urgency

The President offers reassurance and speaks publicly; Rolf interrupts to ask for the practical equipment he needs.

Contrast: the scene sets official language beside Rolf’s immediate focus on Azucena. It sharpens the conflict between public response and the concrete rescue problem.
Question: What does Rolf’s interruption reveal about his priorities at this point?
